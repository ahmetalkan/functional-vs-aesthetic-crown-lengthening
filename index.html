Functional vs aesthetic crown lengthening: a brief for the consult

The same name on a quote can mean two different operations: exposing enough tooth so a crown can seal, or reshaping several front teeth for a gummy smile. This brief is a way to walk into a periodontal consult already knowing which question you are asking, and which numbers on a US planning range belong to which job.

The American Academy of Periodontology’s patient pages are the specialty’s public starting point for gum and supporting-bone procedures. NIDCR describes periodontal disease as an infection of the tissues that hold teeth in place, often beginning with plaque. The ADA’s periodontitis topic reports, from NHANES 2009–2014, that roughly 42% of dentate US adults aged 30 or older have some form of periodontitis. Those sources explain why a periodontist may be involved; they do not, by themselves, tell you whether your case is functional or aesthetic.

The functional job, in millimetres

A guide to when crown lengthening is needed calls the 3 mm rule the practical test: the crown margin needs about 1 mm of sound tooth, 1 mm of attached gum and 1 mm of connective-tissue attachment above the bone. The procedure is indicated when a margin would sit within about 2 mm of the bone, or when a fracture or cavity extends below the gum. The 2026 planning range on that page is $700 to $1,800 for one tooth with gum and bone recontouring, and $350 to $900 for a gum-only reshape. The site’s homepage repeats the one-tooth gum-and-bone band and notes that a gum-only reshape can cost about a third of a bone case.

The aesthetic job is a different quote

The page on aesthetic crown lengthening says the work typically treats four to six upper teeth and costs $2,000–$5,000, with local anaesthesia as standard and sedation optional. If part of the surgery has a restorative reason, that portion may be documented separately; the aesthetic portion is normally the patient’s cost. The related guide to crown lengthening for a gummy smile is careful about limits: the procedure helps when excess gum or high bone covers the teeth, and fails when the lip or the jaw is the cause. Ask which of those three the examination actually found, before you compare a $2,000–$5,000 band with a one-tooth functional fee.

Questions to take in writing

  • Is this visit about a crown seal on one or two teeth, or about a gummy smile across several front teeth?
  • Will bone be recontoured, or is a gum-only reshape being proposed, and why?
  • What is the total, including any later crown or veneer, and which part is likely to be billed as aesthetic?
  • What happens if the lip, not the gum, is the main reason the teeth look short?

Planning ranges are not quotes. Crown Lengthening Cost Guide collects those ranges and a directory of periodontal practices compiled from public business data, which is useful after this brief is filled in.

This is not dental advice and not a diagnosis of biologic-width violation. Only an examination can say whether lengthening, a different restoration, or extraction is the indicated next step.
